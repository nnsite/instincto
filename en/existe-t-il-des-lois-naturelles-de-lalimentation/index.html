Do natural laws of diet exit ?

“Man is a social animal”, “everything depends on the culture”, “everything is only a matter of conditioning” …

Cultural relativism has a good back: as everything is relative to the conditioning undergone by the individual, this one adapts to everything. It would therefore be absurd to speak of natural laws of human behavior, especially in terms of food.

I’m not exaggerating: this language has been spoken to me by many pseudo-philosophers of gastronomy, during conferences or television shows, and I have not always found the most blunt lines on the spot. .

But let’s leave the controversies there, and try to see what exactly is involved. First, it is quite clear that the human organism needs food to survive. Ultimately, it can be satisfied for a certain time with infusions, which are supposed to provide it with essential nutrients and water without which all life is impossible. One can also feed an individual by gastric tube, give him pills concentrating all the necessary food components, but these artifacts are only applicable for relatively short periods of time. Astronauts themselves dream of fresh produce after a stay in an orbital station, and nutritionists who take care of their food ensure a minimum intake of fruits and greens, as expensive as it costs to transport a kilo of goods by space shuttle.

More trivially still, if we deprive an individual of all food, he collapses. Except a few yogis or a few saints, who claim to feed on the zeitgeist or the rays of the sun. We have never been able to prove that the integral fast which they claim was not interspersed with clandestine food supplies. However, normal men like us need food to survive. This alone shows that there are certain natural laws, the first of which is that the organism must be nourished in order to perform its functions.

Can we then take the next step and say that the body must be fed properly to be able to perform its functions proper? The thing seems obvious to us today, but there was a time when the fashion wanted that one attributes to the man a total freedom: what he ate had no influence on his health, the essential was the the pleasure he found there, the social bond, the conviviality, the symbols represented by the dishes and not their biochemical content.

I remember a comedian from Lausanne who, after my first major lectures, gave me a headline: “Me, Mr. Burger, I do not feed on molecules!”. So much determination had shaken me in my intuitions, because it was not at all customary, at the time, to think that food could contain harmful molecules resulting from culinary preparations. However, this is widely demonstrated today.

Already in 1917, the American chemist discovered Maillard, looking for substances that produced the characteristic flavor of roasted potatoes, noted that all kinds of molecules are formed under the effect of heat. The “Maillard molecules” have however remained in the shadows and it is only after the Second World War that the work multiplies slowly. Then at the end of the century, research on diabetes led to the definition of AGEs (Advanced Glycation Endproducts), molecules resulting from chemical reactions between proteins and glucose, forming in the blood in the presence of too high blood sugar.

But it was still another decade before we realized that most of these AGEs are formed during culinary chemical reactions, that they pass into the blood and can cause all kinds of damage. The case of acrylamide, discovered in 2002 for example, marked a new stage: we discovered in crisps, fries and other delicacies resulting from intensive heat treatments spectacular quantities of this molecule which we knew to be toxic: l WHO had long limited its use in disinfecting water to 1 µg per liter, while the doses in the food examined exceeded a thousand times the limit in question. We have of course made arrangements since then to calm the populations.

Several research teams, however, continue to track molecules produced by culinary chemical reactions. Recent publications have just defined ALE (Advanced Lipid Endproducts), which this time are fats altered by the heating of food, the toxicity of which is beyond doubt.

The simple fact that molecules produced by culinary artifacts are toxic demonstrates that there are natural laws of food, that is to say, limits beyond which food can be harmful to health. This follows from the definition of a natural law: it describes a condition necessary for maintaining health, or sufficient to induce functional disorders of the organism.

The natural law that is immediately stated, as a result of the presence of toxic molecules from cooking, is that it is not natural to cook food, especially at high temperature. Or: that foods processed by the application of high temperatures do not correspond to the genetic data of the human organism.

My detractors have long opposed this idea, arguing that cooking at moderate temperatures is harmless, since it causes much fewer chemical reactions. Unfortunately, more recent work shows that AGEs are already synthesized in large numbers around 100 ° C, which involves cooking with water, pressure cookers, and even cooking with steam.

Common sense also suggests from the production of AGEs in the blood of diabetics, where they were first discovered, that temperatures of 37 ° C are sufficient to cause glycation reactions (binding of glucose to d ‘other molecules), whose toxicity explained the degenerations of all kinds resulting from an excessive blood sugar level. It is therefore logically not only the temperature that determines the production of AGEs, but also the glucose concentration. Now, the devil if we often use sugar (or starches, which are chains of glucose) in culinary preparations …

In the end, if cooking generates harmful molecules and if on the other hand raw food is sufficient to ensure health, we can state the following natural law: the human organism needs raw food to ensure its functioning. correct.

However, it is not only the molecular nature of foods that counts, but also their organoleptic characteristics: when a useful product is bad in taste, it is eaten in less quantity. When a harmful product is too attractive, there is a tendency to overeat it. We can therefore logically ask ourselves what happens when we prepare food in such a way that it is as pleasant as possible on the palate. And conversely, what would be the effects of a diet avoiding any preparation improving natural flavors?

Only experience can answer these questions: it suffices to compare the nutritional balance with a prepared food, and that which is set up with a food without preparation, as instinctonutrition wants it. The first method, tested universally on humanity, as in the laboratory on some guinea pig cages, results in a significant percentage of overweight or obesity. The second, on the contrary, ensures a regular balance of weight, provided that one is attentive to one’s body.

A more precise criterion is that of inflammation: with an unprepared diet, the inflammatory tendency is maintained spontaneously within limits such as the classic signs of inflammation, in particular edema, erythema and pain, n ’ not appear in case of injury. This phenomenon also makes it possible to define a whole series of rules which must be respected, and which, for the most part, consist in getting rid of bad habits acquired in the previous culinary context.

This authorizes the statement of another natural law, complementary to the preceding ones: the human organism needs raw and unprepared food to spontaneously ensure a correct nutritional balance.

The definition of the natural laws of food is therefore essentially based on empirical observation: it is by recognizing certain limits whose transgression induces functional disorders that we can define the feeding conditions guaranteeing the correct functioning of the organization. These limits are determined by genetics more than by cultural conditioning, as shown by their universality and independence from previous types of diet.

Instinctotherapy ultimately only defines these natural laws and teaches their application, in particular the various causes of involuntary transgression that could compromise its beneficial effects.

4 reasons to question medicine

  • Because a 100% natural food hygiene could exert a preventive or curative action as yet unsuspected against many diseases, as research has shown for cardiovascular and cancerous diseases, responsible for 70% of deaths in developed countries.
  • Because processed foods thwart the mechanisms of smell and taste, genetically predicted for natural products; mixed with sugars and fats, enhanced by salt and spices, they are abnormally attractive and induce nutritional imbalances, leading to overweight, obesity, diabetes, etc.
  • Because a better nutritional balance ensures that the body receives the substances it needs to maintain or restore its integrity; a better general condition promotes the natural defenses and contributes to the work of healing and the prevention of relapses.
  • Because cooking and mixing food causes chemical reactions. These generate denatured molecules (or New Chemical Species), some of which escape the mechanisms of assimilation, genetically predicted for natural molecules; non-degradable substances (Maillard molecules, AGE, ALE, etc.) can accumulate and produce various disorders, malaise, immune disorders, nervous disorders, premature aging and DNA degeneration.

Example: in the middle of the last century, medicine denied a priori any relationship between diet and cancer. Then, in the 1980s, a large epidemiological study by the WHO revealed a significant link between types of diet and types of cancer. Yet many oncologists today avoid the subject, or minimize the usefulness of a healthy diet, for example in the prevention of relapse.

The advantage of correcting food hygiene rather than medication, when this is not essential, is the absence of toxic side effects. The list of drugs that have had to be withdrawn from circulation is growing every year. Inventing a new molecule to obtain this or that therapeutic effect is very often a DIY rather than a fundamental one, and we must realize afterwards that thousands of patients have suffered from adverse effects that no one had been able to foresee.

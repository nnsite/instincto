Medicine versus instinctotherapy

(⚠ Unfinished)

In order to illustrate the way in which institutions react to disturbing facts, I will just cite in passing an article concerning a very astonishing case of regression of metastases in an undifferentiated trophoblastic teratoma1. This article, recounting the position taken by the Swiss League against cancer, told that a certain Jean-Jacques Besuchet would have started instinctotherapy three weeks before being operated, would then have, always instinctively, developed two pulmonary metastases, then that these metastases would have disappeared spontaneously, to give way six years later to mediastinal cancer of an undiagnosed nature. The conclusion is that the diet could not explain the disappearance of the metastases, which appeared instinctively, and that the new cancer could have revealed a collapse of the defenses after six years of raw diet.

However, the reality is quite different: As he recounts in his book Cap sur la vie2, Besuchet contacted my Food Ecology Center at the time three weeks before the operation. But he didn’t make the decision to change his diet, being convinced that medicine would get him out of the woods. A year and a half later, despite a long and painful chemotherapy, the X-rays revealed the two pulmonary metatstases. Feeling cornered, he then decided to apply the method, and saw his metastases gradually melt away, so that there was no trace left after nine months of strict instinct. Without recurrence after five years, his doctors confirmed to him that the cure was acquired.

A few years later still, destabilized by a difficult love affair (his partner demanded that he resume normal food), he abandoned his instinct. After a year of conventional feeding, he developed a new cancer (a carcinoma, unrelated to his teratoma), duly diagnosed by his doctor, and died in a suicidal attitude linked to his heartache.

  1. Jallut O. [Instinct therapy–raw food with meat with exclusion of milk products. Report no. 16]. Schweizerische Rundschau fur Medizin Praxis = Revue suisse de medecine Praxis. 1989;78:697–701. ↩︎

  2. Besuchet J-J. Cap sur la vie l’histoire d’une guérison. Scarabée; 1984. ↩︎
